# How precise the payback can be

**Date:** 14 November 2026. **Agreed with:** Timi.

## Problem

The sample report showed a payback of "3–10 years": too wide to be useful. It came from combining
the extremes (cheapest price with the highest saving, dearest with the lowest), which almost never
happen together.

## Decision

- **Combine the uncertainties statistically**, not the extremes (price and saving uncertainties added
  in quadrature on a log scale). The sample: 3–10 → about 4–8 years.
- **Use the person's own data**: use from the bills and the real electricity price from what they
  paid; feed-in price from the country table. The sample: about 4–7 years.
- **Partner companies come later.** When the person names a partner company (a next feature, not
  built yet), the equipment price is nearly exact and the payback narrows to about 5–6 years in the
  sample. **Until then the payback is less precise**, and that is accepted.
- **Show:** whole years. Where the range would still be very wide, one number ("about 6 years") with
  the missing data listed. Where there is almost no saving: "does not pay back within 15 years" plus
  the CO₂ saved, never a meaningless range.

## Open

- A "budget / mid-range / premium" equipment question would also narrow the price before partner
  companies exist. Offered, not decided.
